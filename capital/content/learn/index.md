# Learn Capital

## You don't need to invest to start understanding investment.

This is a short, educational primer for asking better questions. It is not personalised financial advice.

## 01 — What are you actually buying?

Start with the underlying asset, ownership structure, rights, obligations, and the documents that establish them.

## 02 — How does a payment plan work?

A staged payment plan divides an obligation across agreed dates or milestones. The dates, conditions, fees, and consequences of a missed payment need to be understood from the actual terms.

## 03 — What does ROI actually mean?

Return on investment is a comparison between an outcome and the amount committed. Historical performance describes what happened; projected performance describes an assumption about what might happen; neither is an actual outcome in advance.

## 04 — What makes an opportunity attractive?

Location, demand, price, financing, liquidity, ownership, operating costs, development risk, exit assumptions, and evidence all matter. No single factor makes a specific opportunity attractive without investigation.

## 05 — What can go wrong?

Construction delays, market changes, liquidity constraints, financing risk, currency movements, unexpected costs, changing demand, and assumptions failing can all change an outcome.

## 06 — How can multiple investors participate?

Collective participation can be modelled conceptually, but its legal structure, suitability, obligations, and protections depend on the actual jurisdiction and arrangement. The Capital site does not provide such a service.

## Ready to see a real opportunity?

Learning comes first. The next step is to examine a research subject with its sources and open questions.
